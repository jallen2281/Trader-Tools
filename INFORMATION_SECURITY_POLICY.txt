--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This policy states how information security risk is identified, mitigated and
monitored for Trader Tools: a personal financial dashboard that stores account
balances, debts, income, spending, investment holdings, tax documents, and — where
a user chooses to connect one — data retrieved from financial institutions
through Plaid.

It applies to the application, the database, the Kubernetes cluster it runs on,
the source repository, the CI/CD pipeline, and every third party that receives
data.

It is written for an operation of one. Controls that presuppose an organization
are marked NOT APPLICABLE rather than reworded to look satisfied; a policy that
claims apparatus it does not have is worse than one that admits the gap.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The sole operator is accountable for every control in this document: maintaining
it, verifying that automated enforcement is running, performing the reviews
defined here, and responding to incidents.

There are no employees, contractors, or other parties with access to production
systems or stored data. Consequently there is no separation of duties, and no
onboarding, offboarding or security-awareness-training apparatus. This is stated
as a fact about the operation's size, not as a control.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. DATA CLASSIFICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  SENSITIVE - handled with the strongest controls in this document
    - Tax documents (W-2, 1099, 1098) which routinely contain Social Security
      numbers, employer details and full wage information
    - Plaid access tokens: long-lived bearer credentials for a bank account
    - Session tokens and Personal Access Tokens

  CONFIDENTIAL - private to a user, never public
    - Account balances, debts, income, budgets, spending transactions
    - Investment holdings, trades, cost basis
    - Household membership and sharing arrangements

  INTERNAL
    - Application logs, operational metrics

  PUBLIC
    - The landing page, privacy policy and terms of service

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. RISK MANAGEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Risks are identified from three sources and recorded with their treatment in
Section 14:

  a) REVIEW - the annual review defined in Section 13, and the access review in
     the Access Control Policy.
  b) CHANGE - every material change to authentication, data handling, third-party
     processors or deployment is assessed for security impact before release.
  c) OPERATION - failures surfaced by logging, failed jobs, or provider alerts.

The principal risks accepted and treated by this policy are: unauthorized access
to another user's financial data; theft of stored bank credentials; exposure of
tax documents containing Social Security numbers; loss of data; and
misconfiguration during deployment.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. ACCESS CONTROL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Governed in full by ACCESS_CONTROL_POLICY.txt. In summary: authentication is
delegated to Google, so no password is ever created or stored, and multi-factor
authentication is enforced at the identity provider. Authorization is role-based
with least privilege — a newly registered account holds no permissions at all.
Visibility of another person's records requires an explicit, per-record share by
that record's owner. Bank credentials and tax profiles are never shareable by any
mechanism.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. DATA PROTECTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

IN TRANSIT
  All traffic is served over HTTPS with certificates issued and renewed
  automatically, and plain HTTP is redirected. Traffic to third-party providers
  is over TLS.

AT REST
  Plaid access tokens are stored ONLY as ciphertext, encrypted with a key held in
  the environment and never in the database, so a database dump does not yield a
  usable bank credential. The encryption key is deliberately separate from the
  application's session key so the two do not share a rotation cycle or a blast
  radius. The application refuses to begin a bank connection at all if that key
  is absent, rather than storing a token it cannot protect.

  Section 14 records where encryption at rest is NOT yet applied.

SECRETS
  Credentials are injected at runtime as Kubernetes Secrets. They are never
  committed to source control and never built into container images; only
  empty-valued templates are versioned. Third-party credentials (AI providers,
  Plaid) live in separately managed secrets from the application's own.

  The application refuses to start in production if the session signing key is
  unset or still the built-in development value, because a known key makes every
  session cookie forgeable.

SERVING USER CONTENT
  Uploaded documents are type-checked on upload and re-validated on download.
  Anything outside the allowlist is served as an opaque attachment rather than
  rendered, with nosniff and a restrictive Content-Security-Policy, so
  user-supplied bytes cannot execute in the application's origin.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. SECURE DEVELOPMENT AND CHANGE MANAGEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

All production changes are made through a GitOps pipeline: changes are committed
to source control, built into an immutable image, and deployed by an automated
controller. Every production change therefore has an auditable, attributable
history, and manual edits to live cluster objects are reverted by the controller
rather than persisting silently.

An automated test suite covers authentication and consent gating, permission
enforcement, record-level sharing boundaries, retention and deletion, document
serving, database schema migration, and third-party integration behaviour. Tests
are run before release. Negative assertions are treated as first-class: sharing
and permission tests assert what a user must NOT be able to see or do, because
those failures are silent.

Database schema changes are applied automatically at startup by comparing the
models against the live schema, so a forgotten migration cannot leave the
application querying columns that do not exist.

Configuration defaults fail safe: debug mode is off unless explicitly enabled,
and authentication fails closed in production if the auth stack does not
initialize.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. THIRD-PARTY MANAGEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every third party receiving data is named in the published privacy policy, with
what it receives and why. They are currently: Google (authentication), Anthropic
and Google AI (analysis of financial summaries, and extraction of uploaded
documents), Plaid (financial account connections), and market data providers
(ticker symbols only, no personal data).

Before any new processor is added, the privacy policy is updated to name it and
what it receives. Data minimization is applied: analysis requests send summarized
figures rather than whole records, and document extraction is user-initiated and
can be declined in favour of manual entry.

Access credentials for third parties are held in dedicated secrets and can be
rotated independently.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. LOGGING AND MONITORING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The application logs requests, responses and errors, including a full traceback
for startup failures. Security-relevant events are logged explicitly: consent
being recorded, bank connections being made and revoked, retention purges, and
migration failures.

Liveness and readiness are reported separately. A pod whose authentication stack
failed to initialize reports itself NOT READY and is removed from service, so a
broken release stalls on healthy previous instances instead of replacing them
with instances that cannot authenticate anyone.

Section 14 records the gap in log retention verification.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. INCIDENT RESPONSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

On discovering a suspected compromise:

  1. CONTAIN. Rotate the affected credential immediately — the session signing
     key, the Plaid token encryption key, third-party API keys, or database
     credentials as applicable. Rotating the session key invalidates all sessions;
     rotating the Plaid encryption key requires users to reconnect institutions.
  2. REVOKE. Disconnect affected Plaid items, which revokes the token at the
     provider, and revoke outstanding Personal Access Tokens.
  3. ASSESS. Determine which data classification was involved, whose data, and
     over what period, using the logs in Section 9.
  4. NOTIFY. Where sensitive data as defined in Section 3 was or may have been
     exposed, notify affected users directly and without delay, and notify Plaid
     if any connected-account data was involved.
  5. REMEDIATE AND RECORD. Fix the cause, add a regression test where the cause
     was a software defect, and record the incident and its resolution in
     Section 15.

Contact for security reports: admin@kegbot.net.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11. BACKUP AND CONTINUITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The production database is backed up on an automated nightly schedule with a
30-day retention window, older dumps being pruned automatically.

Restoration has not been rehearsed. An untested backup is an assumption rather
than a control, and this is recorded as a gap in Section 14.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12. PHYSICAL AND ENVIRONMENTAL SECURITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The application does not run on rented cloud capacity. It runs on physical server
hardware owned outright by the operator, colocated in a commercial datacenter
that maintains PCI DSS, HIPAA and government compliance programs.

That matters because the physical and environmental layer is therefore covered by
independently audited controls rather than by assertion. Facilities holding those
programs are required to operate — and to evidence, to an auditor — controlled
and logged entry, video monitoring with defined retention, visitor escort and
authorization procedures, and power, cooling and fire suppression redundancy.
Attestation documents (for example a SOC 2 report or a PCI Attestation of
Compliance) can be requested from the facility and supplied as evidence where a
counterparty needs it.

There is also no shared tenancy and no hypervisor neighbour, which removes an
entire class of risk that rented capacity carries.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT MEAN
  The facility's certifications are the FACILITY's, not this application's. Under
  the shared responsibility model that colocation implies, the datacenter is
  accountable for physical and environmental security; the operator remains
  accountable for everything above it — the operating system, the cluster, the
  database, the application, and every control in the rest of this policy.

  Trader Tools is NOT PCI DSS or HIPAA compliant, and does not claim to be.
  Neither regime applies to it in any case: the application handles no cardholder
  data and no protected health information. Housing a server in an audited
  facility does not transfer that audit to the software running on it, and
  representing otherwise to a counterparty would be a material misstatement.

RESIDUAL PHYSICAL RISK
  Facility personnel can physically reach the hardware. This is inherent to
  colocation, and in a facility of this standard that access is authorized,
  logged and monitored rather than open — which substantially bounds the risk
  but does not eliminate it, because physical possession of a drive defeats every
  software access control above it.

  The operator is also employed by the facility. That is disclosed here because a
  control framework should record where operational and custodial roles overlap,
  rather than leave a reviewer to discover it.

  The tenant-side control for this residual risk is encryption at rest, and it is
  the operator's responsibility, not the facility's. It is also what both PCI DSS
  and HIPAA would themselves require for stored sensitive data. This is why the
  unencrypted tax documents and unencrypted backups recorded in Section 14 remain
  the highest-priority remediation items in this policy: they are the one place
  where a well-controlled physical environment still leaves Social Security
  numbers readable to anyone holding the disk.

Hardware disposal: drives are securely erased or destroyed before any machine
leaves the operator's custody, and are never returned to a vendor or resold
carrying application data.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
13. POLICY REVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This policy is reviewed at least ANNUALLY, and additionally whenever:

  - a new category of personal data begins to be collected;
  - a new third-party processor begins receiving data;
  - the authentication or authorization model changes;
  - the deployment or hosting model changes;
  - an incident occurs.

Each review confirms that the controls described here still match what the system
does, that automated enforcement is running, and that Section 14 is current.
Outcomes are recorded in Section 15, including reviews resulting in no change.
